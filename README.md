# Early-Life Environment and Adult Health in China: Recalled Childhood Neighborhood Conditions, Historical Air Pollution, and Self-Rated versus Measured Health

## Abstract

Early-life environmental conditions, including air pollution, may affect health decades later, but survey evidence often relies on respondents’ reports of both exposure and health. This study compares associations of recalled childhood neighborhood conditions with self-rated health and measured physical function using the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS), its 2014 life-history survey and 2015 physical examinations. Among 13,720 main respondents, self-rated health and peak expiratory flow are standardized within the same sample and regressed on identical covariates. Their difference is tested with community-clustered inference and cluster resampling. A favorable recalled neighborhood is associated with 0.115 SD better self-rated health but no detectable peak-flow difference (−0.010 SD; 95% CI −0.042 to 0.021). The coefficient difference is 0.126 SD (95% CI 0.079 to 0.172). It persists under measurement-quality restrictions, selection and survey weighting, and in 2011 and 2013 health outcomes measured before the recall interview. On a fixed sample, adding depressive symptoms and measured and reported health reduces the self-rated-health association from 4.89 to 2.50 percentage points; measured indicators alone change it little. Reported physician-diagnosed lung disease shows an intermediate pattern. Historical total suspended particulate (TSP) exposure estimates linked to 6,539 younger records likewise show no detectable association with reported health, mostly parent-reported offspring health, in Probit or instrumental-variable Probit models. These estimates are imprecise, the birth-year instruments are weak, and the sample is not directly comparable with the main respondents. Recalled childhood environments therefore relate differently to different dimensions of adult health; whether the difference reflects psychological health, broader physical health or reporting remains unidentified.

## Data availability

This study uses data from the China Health and Retirement Longitudinal Study (CHARLS); the dataset is not publicly redistributed in this repository, and users must apply independently to CHARLS for access.
